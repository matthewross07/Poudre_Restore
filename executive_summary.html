1. Executive Summary & Core Principle

Restoration Without Monitoring is Greenwashing: Stream restoration across snowmelt gravel rivers often relies on structural channel modifications that yield visual physical complexity on Day 1 but fail to improve downstream water quality or ecosystem metrics 5 years post-construction (Bernhardt et al. 2005). We advocate instituting the "3% Sentinel Rule": mandating that 3–5% of capital project budgets be dedicated to automated, real-time sensor monitoring (Temp, DO, Turbidity, NO3⁻) to provide unassailable proof of return on investment.

3. Committee Strategic Guidance & Recommendations
Q1. Incorporating Monitoring Mindfully
Answer: Institute the "3% Sentinel Rule" requiring 3–5% of capital project budgets for automated continuous sensor stations (Temp, DO, Turbidity, NO3⁻) over sparse monthly grab samples.
Q2. Restoration & Investment Strategy
Answer: Prioritize low-overhead, permanent returns (flow preservation, canopy shading, diversion consolidations) over high-maintenance structural channel fixes.
Q3. Prioritizing the 5 Criteria
Answer: Focus on Water Flows & Aquatic Habitat via Barrier Removal between Munroe Canal & South Platte to solve passage, thermal & habitat bottlenecks together.
Q4. Best Projects for Flow Regime
Answer: Diversion consolidation, seasonal water-sharing leases, and automated bypass gates. Channel tweaks cannot replace missing summer water.
Q5. Committee's Biggest Bang for Buck
Answer: Riparian canopy re-establishment (willow/cottonwood shading) paired with diversion dam fish-passage & flow retrofits.
Q6. Key Stakeholders to Engage
Answer: Irrigation ditch companies (for flow consolidation opportunities) and independent open-data hydroinformatics teams.
